# Pews founding-church interest push

## Objective

Find out whether real church leaders have a painful, recurring operations problem that Pews can earn the right to solve. The first push is for conversations and qualified fit checks—not traffic, followers, or a bloated “waitlist.”

Public page: https://warpapaya.github.io/pews-early-access/

## Who to contact first

Start with 10–15 people who know Petie or have a credible warm introduction:

1. Executive pastors, church administrators, and operations leaders who feel cross-ministry handoff pain.
2. Worship or ministry leaders who coordinate services and volunteers every week.
3. Lead pastors at small and midsize churches who currently carry operations themselves.
4. People who recently changed church-management tools or openly complain about tool sprawl.

Do not start with a mass list. Pews has founder credibility but no customer proof yet; personal context is the advantage.

## First two-week sequence

- Days 1–3: 5 highly personal notes. Ask for frank feedback, not a favor-share.
- Days 4–7: 5–10 additional warm notes, adjusted using the language from the first replies.
- Days 7–10: ask responsive contacts for one relevant introduction.
- Days 10–14: one personal founder post if direct responses show a repeated pain pattern.
- Do not buy ads yet. Paid traffic before message/ICP proof would be a tidy way to purchase ambiguity.

## Decision rules

Continue into structured discovery when the first 15–25 targeted contacts produce:

- at least 5 completed, non-synthetic fit checks;
- at least 3 leaders willing to have a follow-up conversation;
- at least 2 churches describing the same expensive or recurring workflow failure; and
- budget signals that can support a real product, not merely enthusiasm for a free tool.

Revise the page or form when qualified contacts visit but do not complete the fit check. Revise positioning when they complete it but describe unrelated problems. Pause the push if 25 genuinely relevant warm contacts produce fewer than 3 completed fit checks; more traffic will not repair weak demand.

## Truth boundaries

- Call it a private beta or product direction, not a live customer deployment.
- Do not claim customer results, production readiness, or finalized pricing.
- Do not promise every workflow shown will be in the first beta.
- Do not request congregant records, giving records, care notes, credentials, or other sensitive church data during discovery.
- Any church invited to use real data needs a separately agreed beta scope, data boundary, support expectation, and exit/deletion plan.

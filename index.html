Why this approach

“Churches do not need another rip-and-replace project. They need fewer dropped handoffs this week.”

I spent years as a worship pastor before moving into technology. The real friction was rarely a missing database field. It was the handoff between Sunday plans, volunteer needs, guest responses, care conversations, and the people expected to remember all of it.

Pews starts there. It sits above the current stack, makes unresolved work visible, and gives each next action an owner. If it later replaces a module, that should happen because it proved the value—not because a roadmap slide demanded it.

Before you request access

Clear answers, while the product is still early.

What will the Founding Ops Beta cost?

$39 per month or $399 prepaid for one year. Requesting access is free and does not enroll or charge your church. Scope, start date, billing, data access, and exit terms will be agreed before participation begins.

Is this a Planning Center replacement?

Not in the first phase. Planning Center stays in place. Pews is being tested as an operational layer that can surface selected signals and carry Pews-owned follow-ups. Broader replacement is a later decision, workflow by workflow.

What is actually in the first phase?

A weekly operations view, owners and due dates, guest and care follow-ups, service and volunteer-gap visibility, and limited read-oriented connection work where source access allows. The exact first workflow is agreed with each church.

What is explicitly out of scope?

Full migration, donation processing, accounting, check-in hardware, native worship planning, mass messaging, and a finished member app are not part of the Founding Ops Beta unless separately added and proven later.

What happens after I submit?

We will review your answers. If the church looks like a useful fit for the first cohort, we will follow up to understand one concrete weekly workflow and agree on a bounded evaluation. Submission is a request for that conversation—not a guarantee of access or timing.

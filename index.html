Software should clarify the work—not become more of it.

Pews is being shaped around recurring ministry rhythms: preparing services, filling volunteer needs, following up with people, communicating clearly, and keeping stewardship work in view.

01 / Plan

Prepare the service

Create and copy services, work from templates, manage service status, and keep songs and teams close to the plan.

02 / Staff

Surface the gaps

Review unfilled volunteer positions by date and team, search for people, and assign the work that still needs an owner.

Pews is being built from lived ministry experience and twelve years in technology—not from a distance from church operations.

Why this product

“Church software should respect both the people doing the work and the mission funds paying for it.”

I spent years as a worship pastor before moving into technology. That made the weekly reality concrete: plans change, volunteers need clarity, people need care, and the administrative work never stays in one lane.

After twelve years in technology, those lessons are coming back together in Pews: useful operational discipline without asking churches to buy complexity they do not need—or divert unreasonable resources away from the mission.

Petie ClarkFounder of Pews · former worship pastor · technology leader

Before you request access

Clear answers, while the product is still early.

What will the beta cost?

Pricing and the first beta scope are still being validated. Pews is intended to be a paid product, but requesting a conversation does not commit your church to a price. Any scope, timing, and cost will be clear before participation begins.

What parts of church work is Pews exploring?

The current product direction spans people and households, services and songs, volunteer scheduling, care follow-ups, giving and statements, communications, check-ins, groups, events, prayer, media, sermons, streaming, rooms, reports, and administrative settings. The first private-beta scope will be narrower and agreed with each participating church.

What happens after I submit?

Petie will review your answers. If your church looks like a useful fit for the first group, he will follow up to learn more and agree on a narrow evaluation. Submission is a request for that conversation—not a guarantee of access, timing, or a particular feature set.
